Every film has a director, and every director was born somewhere. I mapped the birthplaces of {{source.n_iso|int}} film directors in Wikidata to see which cities made them, how the map has moved since the silent era, who left home to make films, and how long it took for women to appear.

Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, knows {{source.n|int}} people who have directed at least one film. Some are household names, most are not: silent-era directors of one-reel comedies, documentary makers, television people who made a single feature, actors and musicians who tried directing once. For {{source.n_iso|int}} of them it records where they were born, and for {{source.n_pt|int}} that birthplace has coordinates.

So I put them all on one map. The interactive version has a decade slider, a filter for women and for well-known directors, a search box, and lines showing who made their films in another country. Click any hexagon and you get the directors born there, best known first.

Darker hexagons hold more directors.}}

A city of directors

Wikidata often records a district rather than a city: Brooklyn, a Paris arrondissement, Neuilly-sur-Seine. So I grouped every birthplace within 30 km of a big city. New York comes first, with {{areas.top.0.n|int}} directors, then Paris ({{areas.top.1.n|int}}), London ({{areas.top.2.n|int}}), Los Angeles ({{areas.top.3.n|int}}) and Rome ({{areas.top.4.n|int}}). Tehran ({{areas.tehran.n|int}}) is the first city outside Europe and the Americas, ahead of Tokyo.

Per head, the list turns over. Prague has {{areas.per_capita.0.per100k|f1}} directors in Wikidata for every 100,000 people living there now, and Copenhagen {{areas.per_capita.1.per100k|f1}}. Tokyo has {{areas.tokyo.per100k|f1}} and Mumbai {{areas.mumbai.per100k|f1}}. That is partly history, because a director born in 1930 was born in a much smaller city, and partly the record: some countries' film archives have been copied into Wikidata much more thoroughly than others.

Right, per 100,000 people living there now: Prague 45.1, Copenhagen 37.6, Munich 23.1, Stockholm 22.1 and Rome 19.0|Directors born within 30 km of each city, as a count and per 100,000 present-day residents.}}

From Europe and America towards Asia

The United States is the birthplace of {{countries.top.0.n|int}} directors, about one in six of those with a birth country, followed by Germany, France, Italy and India. But the map moves if you sort directors by the decade of their first film. Of those who debuted by the 1950s, {{decades.eu_na_early_pct|pc}} were born in Europe or North America and {{decades.asia_early_pct|pc}} in Asia. Since 2000, the figures are {{decades.eu_na_late_pct|pc}} and {{decades.asia_late_pct|pc}}. India's share doubles, and South Korea and Iran, almost absent in the early decades, each now account for about {{decades.kor.late_pct|f1}}% of new directors.

{{IMG|b_shift.png|Stacked bar chart of directors' continent of birth by the decade of their first film, from before 1910 to the 2020s. Europe is about 60 per cent until the 1960s and about half afterwards; North America is largest in the 1910s; Asia grows from a sliver to about a quarter in the 2000s and 2010s|Where directors were born, by the decade of their first dated film.}}

One industry is missing from that story. Nollywood makes more films than almost anyone, yet Wikidata has a birthplace for only {{coverage.nga_bp_pct|pc}} of the {{coverage.nga_n|int}} directors whose films are mainly Nigerian, so Nigeria barely appears on the map. Its directors are there in the films, just not in the biographies.

Born here, made films there

For each director I took the country that produced most of their films and compared it with where they were born. {{migration.pct|pc}}, about one in seven, made their films mainly in another country, and that share has hardly changed in a hundred years. The biggest flow is from the United Kingdom to the United States ({{migration.flows.0.n|int}} directors, from Charlie Chaplin to Ridley Scott), then Canada to the United States ({{migration.flows.1.n|int}}), and Austria and Poland to Germany. Of directors whose films are mainly American, {{migration.us_pct|pc}} were born abroad, and {{migration.us_1930.pct|pc}} of those who debuted in the 1930s, when Hollywood was full of directors from Berlin, Vienna and Budapest.

The thickest runs from the United Kingdom to the United States, others from Canada, Germany and France to the United States, from Austria and Poland to Germany, and from Algeria to France. A box lists the largest flows: UK to US 416, Canada to US 232, Austria to Germany 124, Poland to Germany 117|The largest flows of directors from their birth country to the country of most of their films.}}

Where the women went

The first film director in this data who was a woman is also one of the first directors of all: Alice Guy-Blaché, who made her first film in Paris in the 1890s. Lois Weber, Mabel Normand and Gene Gauntier directed in America in the 1910s, Anna Hoffman-Uddgren in Sweden and Luise Fleck in Vienna. Then the door closed. Women were {{women.low_dec.pct|f1}}% of directors who debuted in the 1920s, and stayed below 5% in every decade until the 1970s. Since then the share has climbed: {{women.d2010.pct|pc}} in the 2010s and {{women.d2020.pct|pc}} in the 2020s so far.

{{IMG|b_women.png|Line chart of women's share of directors by the decade of their first film, flat at around 3 to 5 per cent from the 1910s to the 1960s, then rising steadily to 32 per cent in the 2020s; a dashed line for notable directors follows a similar path and reaches 43 per cent|Women's share of directors, by debut decade, for all directors and for notable ones.}}

Women were {{women.low_dec.pct|f1}}% of directors who debuted in the 1920s. In the 2020s they are {{women.d2020.pct|pc}}.

Women's birthplaces follow the same broad map as men's, and so do the birthplaces of the best-known directors, those with {{meta.notable|int}} or more Wikipedia and other Wikimedia pages, except that fame is even more American: the United States is the birthplace of {{countries.us_notable_pct|pc}} of notable directors, more than twice its share of all.

A map of who keeps records

All of this rests on what Wikidata happens to know. Almost every notable director has a birthplace ({{source.notable_bp_pct|f1}}%), but only {{source.sl1_bp_pct|pc}} of those with one sitelink or none. Directors whose films are mainly Russian, Italian or Iranian usually have one; Canadian, Danish or Nigerian directors usually do not. So the map is best read as the open record of film, rich where archives have been copied in and thin where they have not.

{{IMG|b_coverage.png|Bar chart of the share of directors with a known birthplace by the country that produced most of their films: Hungary 88 per cent, Russia 84, Italy 82, Iran 81 and Poland 80 at the top; China 39, South Korea 37, Australia 34, Nigeria 33, Denmark 32, Canada 31 and Peru 29 at the bottom|How often Wikidata knows where a director was born, by the country of their films.}}

How I did this

Two queries to Wikidata through the QLever service gave every person who directed an item classed as a film, with their birthplace, birth date, gender and number of sitelinks, and every film credit with its release date and countries of origin. I placed each birthplace in its present-day country with Natural Earth outlines, grouped birthplaces within 30 km of cities of a million or more, took each director's first dated film as their debut, and took the country of most of their films as their country of work, treating the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia and other former states as covering all their successors. Everything is in the paper, with the code and data on GitHub.

The usual caveats apply, and here they matter a lot. Wikidata is not a census of film-makers. It includes actors and musicians who directed one film, it records gender for {{source.gender_pct|pc}} of directors and mostly as a binary, and its coverage depends on which national film databases have been imported. Country of birth uses today's borders, so a director born in Lviv in 1900 counts as Ukrainian.
