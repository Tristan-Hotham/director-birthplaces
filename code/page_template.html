Where every film director is from

Wikidata, the open database behind Wikipedia, knows {{source.n|int}} people who have directed a film, and where {{source.n_iso|int}} of them were born. This is their map: the cities that made directors, the slow move from Europe and America towards Asia, the directors who left home to make films somewhere else, and how long it took for women to appear.

Every film has a director, and every director was born somewhere. Put those birthplaces on a map and you get a strange, lopsided picture of the world: crowded around Paris, New York, Rome and Prague, thin across Africa, and changing decade by decade as new film industries grow. It is also a picture of who has been written down. Wikidata records a birthplace for {{source.bp_pct|pc}} of its directors, almost all of the famous ones and only a quarter of those with one sitelink or none.

So read the map as the open record of film, not a census of it. Drag the decade slider to see where the directors who debuted in each decade were born, filter to women, or to well-known directors only (those with {{meta.notable|int}} or more Wikipedia and other Wikimedia pages), search for a name or a city, and click a hexagon for the directors born there.

    02 · CitiesNew York, Paris and the city of directors

    Group every birthplace within 30 km of a big city together, so that Brooklyn counts with New York and Neuilly with Paris, and {{areas.top.0.name|raw}} comes first with {{areas.top.0.n|int}} directors, then {{areas.top.1.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.1.n|int}}), {{areas.top.2.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.2.n|int}}), {{areas.top.3.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.3.n|int}}) and {{areas.top.4.name|raw}} ({{areas.top.4.n|int}}). Just ten cities account for {{areas.top10_pct|pc}} of all directors on the map. Tehran ({{areas.tehran.n|int}}) is the first city outside Europe and the Americas, ahead of Tokyo ({{areas.tokyo.n|int}}).

Part of that is history, since today's population is a poor guide to the city a director was born in decades ago, and part is the record: Wikidata is far fuller for some film cultures, Czech, Danish, Swedish, than for the cinemas of India or China.

    03 · CountriesOne in six was born in the United States

    The United States is the birthplace of {{countries.top.0.n|int}} directors, {{countries.us_pct|pc}} of everyone with a birth country, followed by Germany ({{countries.top.1.n|int}}), France ({{countries.top.2.n|int}}), Italy ({{countries.top.3.n|int}}) and India ({{countries.top.4.n|int}}). Europe as a whole accounts for {{countries.europe_pct|pc}}, Asia {{countries.by_cont.2.pct|pc}} and Africa {{countries.africa_pct|f1}}%. Switch to notable directors and the American share more than doubles, to {{countries.us_notable_pct|pc}}: fame, as Wikipedia measures it, is even more concentrated than film-making.

    04 · The shiftFrom Europe and America towards Asia

    Sort directors by the decade of their first film and the map moves. Of directors who debuted by the 1950s, {{decades.eu_na_early_pct|pc}} were born in Europe or North America and {{decades.asia_early_pct|pc}} in Asia. Among those who debuted since 2000, Europe and North America are down to {{decades.eu_na_late_pct|pc}} and Asia is up to {{decades.asia_late_pct|pc}}. India's share doubles from {{decades.ind.early_pct|f1}}% to {{decades.ind.late_pct|f1}}%, South Korea's grows from {{decades.kor.early_pct|f1}}% to {{decades.kor.late_pct|f1}}% and Iran's from {{decades.irn.early_pct|f1}}% to {{decades.irn.late_pct|f1}}%, while the United States falls from {{decades.us_early_pct|pc}} to {{decades.us_late_pct|pc}}.

    05 · Away from homeDirectors who made their films abroad

    For each director I took the country that produced most of their films and compared it with where they were born. {{migration.pct|pc}} of {{migration.n|int}} directors made their films mainly in another country. The biggest single flow is from the {{migration.flows.0.from_name|raw}} to the {{migration.flows.0.to_name|raw}} ({{migration.flows.0.n|int}} directors, Charlie Chaplin and Ridley Scott among them), then from {{migration.flows.1.from_name|raw}} to the {{migration.flows.1.to_name|raw}} ({{migration.flows.1.n|int}}), and from Austria and Poland to Germany. China to Hong Kong and Algeria to France follow the routes of history.

    06 · WomenFrom {{women.low_dec.pct|f1}}% to {{women.d2020.pct|pc}}

    Alice Guy-Blaché made her first film in Paris in the 1890s, and the early silent era had a handful of women directors: Lois Weber, Mabel Normand and Gene Gauntier in America, Anna Hoffman-Uddgren in Sweden, Luise Fleck in Vienna. Then the door closed. Among directors who debuted in the 1920s, {{women.d1920.pct|f1}}% were women, and the share stayed below 5% in every decade until the 1970s. It reached {{women.d1970.pct|f1}}% in the 1970s, {{women.d2010.pct|pc}} in the 2010s and {{women.d2020.pct|pc}} in the 2020s so far.

    08 · The gapsA map of who keeps records

    Notable directors almost always have a birthplace in Wikidata ({{source.notable_bp_pct|f1}}%); directors with one sitelink or none, only {{source.sl1_bp_pct|pc}}. And the gaps are not spread evenly. A director whose films are mainly Russian, Italian or Iranian usually has one; one whose films are mainly Canadian, Danish or Nigerian usually does not, because many entries came in bulk from film databases that list titles and names but not birthplaces.

    09 · How I did thisMethods and caveats

    Directors. One query through QLever returned every Wikidata person who is the director (P57) of at least one item that is an instance of film (Q11424), with their birthplace (P19), its coordinates and country, date of birth, gender, number of sitelinks and number of films: {{source.n_rows|int}} rows for {{source.n|int}} directors. Unknown values were dropped. A second query linked {{source.n_links|int}} director credits on {{source.n_films|int}} films to their publication dates and countries of origin. Because the query asks only for directors of films, it includes actors, musicians and artists who directed one film, and a few bands credited as directors of concert films; {{source.n_nolabel|int}} directors have no English label in the extract.

    Birthplaces and countries. A director's point is their birthplace's coordinates ({{source.n_pt|int}} directors, {{source.n_places|int}} distinct places). {{source.n_country_bp|int}} birthplaces are a whole country and give a country but no point. The birth country is the present-day country the point falls in (Natural Earth); it agrees with the birthplace's Wikidata country for {{source.agree_pct|pc}} of directors. So a director born in Lviv in 1900 counts as Ukrainian. City areas group birthplaces within 30 km of a Natural Earth city of a million people or more ({{source.in_area_pct|pc}} of points), and per-head figures divide by that city's present population: a rough guide only.

    Decades and countries of work. A director's debut decade is that of their earliest dated film in Wikidata ({{source.debut_pct|pc}} have one), which may miss earlier shorts or television work. Their main film country is the country of origin of most of their films, splitting co-productions equally; films of the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, the German states and British India count for every present-day successor when deciding whether a director worked abroad, so a Ukrainian-born Soviet director is not counted as a migrant.

    Gender. Women and trans women are counted as women, men and trans men as men, and other values as another gender. Shares exclude the {{women.u_pct|pc}} of directors with no gender recorded.

    What this is not. It is not a census of film directors, nor a ranking of national cinemas. Coverage is uneven between countries, between decades and between famous and obscure directors, as section 08 shows. Sitelinks measure attention on Wikipedia, not talent: the most linked "directors" include Charlie Chaplin and Alfred Hitchcock, but also Thomas Edison, Madonna and Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
